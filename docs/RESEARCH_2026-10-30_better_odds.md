## 1. GTAA-5 trend sleeve

Why it beats 0DTE for Caspar: adjusted closes are real total-return prices, so the modelled-option trap does not apply. Twelve decisions a year, most months no order, nothing to watch between 21:30 and 04:00. The worst day is diversified beta, not a gap through a short strike. It runs at $10K on Alpaca fractional now and on IBKR whole shares later. Honest caveat: it earns less than a static 60/40 (about 8% vs 9.5% post-2013) and lagged its own assets' buy-and-hold by about 1 point a year. Its edge is drawdown, not return. GEM and single-index rules are out: on daily data GEM matched SPY's -33.7% in 2020.

First experiment: (a) one day offline, replay on daily data 2007-2026 and post-2013 separately, 10 bps per one-way trade, cash at the T-bill rate, rebalance-day sweep (1st, 10th, 20th), gate written before the run. (b) Twelve monthly paper rebalances on Alpaca as a $10K sub-book with its own client_order_id prefix, never touching casaa-* or ZeroDTE legs, with a 60/40 shadow logged. Gate to IBKR: six clean rebalances with fills matching signals, drawdown inside the bar, and a UCITS vs US-listed decision (30% dividend withholding, US$60K estate cliff). Cost: one to two days, zero capital.

## 2. XSP condor as a cost audit

Why it survives: it is the only short-vol structure whose max loss is fixed by construction (5-wide XSP = $500 per lot), it needs 1-4 orders a month, and XSP is on Alpaca paper now. Honest caveat: no public record shows it earning above cash since 2007. The 9.8% from 1986-2006 is a Cboe back-test. Treat it as a measurement of real spreads and credit-to-width, not an income bot.

First experiment: Phase 0 (free, one day): download PUT, CNDR, BFLY and WPUT CSVs, recompute from 2007 with a 2-4 bp per roll friction haircut, apply the gate. When it fails, keep only a shadow logger: each weekday capture the XSP 45-DTE chain (bid, ask, 16-delta short, 5-delta long, credit as a fraction of width, quoted spread per leg). Optional Phase 1: one 1-lot paper condor a week, filled at mid minus half the spread, managed at 50% profit or 21 DTE, 30 cycles (about eight months), kill if expectancy per cycle is at or below zero or any cycle loses more than 60% of max risk.

## 3. Regulated BTC basis, watch only

Why it survives: it is the only family with narrow daily P&L by construction and no directional view. Honest caveat: Binance BTC funding over the last 365 days was 3.46% gross, below bills; quarterly basis is about 5.6% against bills near 4.1%, so the excess is near zero; perps are not legally available to a Singapore resident. First experiment: one support ticket to IBKR Singapore asking whether the account can hold IBIT and trade MBT, and the MBT margin; a cron job logging forward basis minus the 13-week bill. No capital until the gate. Cost: an email and 20 lines of code.

## Rejected because

- NQ Crazy Ivan: self-reported record does not reconcile, no capital base shown, a 7%-OTM MNQ put loses about $12.6K per lot in a 2020-size move; unboundable at $10K.
- Intraday ORB, VWAP, overnight: QQQ ORB drops to Sharpe 0.23 at 2 cents slippage; SPY momentum edge about zero since 2025; 60 sessions cannot tell Sharpe 0.4 from zero.
- VIX and VRP regime timing: the filter's whole benefit is Sep-Oct 2008, it fails its own two-halves test, and it was ON in Jan 2018 and Feb 2020.
- Buy-write, put-write, wheel: real 0.6-beta income (PUT 7.2%, BXM 8.6%) with -32.7% to -35.8% drawdowns, a -17.7% worst month and $76K per XSP lot; revisit at $40-80K, and then it needs no bot.

### intraday-orb-futures  [REFUTED on review]

**Survives as:** What survives is a negative finding. There is no verifiable net-of-cost edge in this family at $10K, and nothing that bounds the worst day. Do not put real money on any of it, and do not spend a 60-session paper test on the strategy for return validation.

The narrow honest version:
1. Overnight close-to-open on SPY is the only leg with a long record that is not an author-run backtest (sr917 for futures; a Substack for the SPY series). Its net edge is smaller than a retail commission, so it only matters if commissions are $0 and the order can be placed as MOC/MOO. It also takes unhedged gap risk, which conflicts with Caspar's worst-day requirement. Treat it as a market-exposure timing choice, not an alpha source.
2. The SPY noise-area rule is the only member with a real out-of-sample question, and two independent sources I can read (the companion repo, and the cost replications for ORB) say the edge is compressed or gone since early 2025. If Caspar wants to run anything here, run it only as an unattended execution and logging test: record signal price versus fill price, charge 1 cent/share on SPY regardless, and judge it on slippage and plumbing, not on return. A 60-session sample cannot tell a Sharpe 0.4 strategy from zero, so the proposed kill criteria are not a test of the edge.
3. Everything else (QQQ/TQQQ ORB, VWAP trend, Gao-style last-half-hour) should be dropped. The unleveraged ORB falls to buy-and-hold or worse under realistic slippage, the VWAP paper has only author numbers plus weaker independent replications, and the Gao-style effect fails out-of-sample on ETFs after a 2 bp cost.

Recommended next step: stop spending effort on this family and put the research budget into a different family where a long public track record exists, such as the CBOE PUT/BXM/CNDR/CLL benchmarks or multi-year trend/GTAA allocations. I did not check those in this review.

What survives from the literature:
- The put-writing premium is real but small. PUT beats a pure beta-matched mix by about 2-3%/yr, with a -32.7% drawdown and a -17.7% worst month. That is not bounded-risk or $10K-friendly.
- Short-VIX products are out: they have unbounded or product-terminating tails.
- The VIX-timing and vol-timing filters are not an edge. The VRP filter's value is two months in 2008, and the term-structure filter shows no mean gain. If used at all, it is a small tail trimmer on a handful of observations.
